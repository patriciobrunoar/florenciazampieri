MINC — Integral Neurocompatible Model

Architecture · Neuroscience · Wellbeing. Learning ecosystems and neurocompatible environments — designing for development, wellbeing and human diversity.

Why It Matters

The environment shapes the developing brain

Today’s childhood faces sensory overload, stress and disconnection from nature. The environment directly impacts the developing nervous system.

“Not all brains perceive the same way. But all brains are affected by the environment.” — Florencia Zampieri | The Blue Maker

Architecture can lower environmental stress and support the development of every child.

Factors Affecting Neurodevelopment

Digital hyperstimulation Acoustic pollution Visual stress Nature deficit Toxic stress and violence Emotional temperature Poor sleep quality Chaotic environments

Principles of Neurocompatible Design

The MINC Model

  • 1. Sensory Regulation. Reducing overstimulation to lower stress and support self-regulation.
  • 2. Biophilia & Nature. Nature regulates, restores and connects.
  • 3. Spatial Neurodiversity. Every child needs different spaces for different ways of learning.
  • 4. Biological Rhythms. The environment should accompany the rhythms of body and brain.
  • 5. Safety & Belonging. A predictable, welcoming and loving environment generates regulation.

Educating for a Healthy Future

  • Good nutrition in pregnancy and infancy.
  • Secure emotional bonds.
  • Free play and movement.
  • Less screen time at early ages.
  • Daily contact with nature.
  • Early detection and support.
  • Healthy, toxin-free environments.
