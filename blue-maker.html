Manifesto

The Blue Maker

We believe that human wellbeing emerges from the interaction between knowledge, perception, experience and environment.

The Blue Maker is an interdisciplinary framework that brings together architecture, neuroscience, environmental psychology, colour, art, education and longevity research to investigate how environments influence human cognition, behaviour and wellbeing.

We study the relationship between the individual and the environments they inhabit, integrating scientific evidence with observation, experimentation and creative practice.

Our approach connects research and application, translating knowledge into new ways of understanding spaces, learning processes, sensory experiences and human development across the lifespan.

We are interested in the mechanisms through which perception becomes experience, experience shapes behaviour, and environments contribute to wellbeing.

We believe that advancing human wellbeing requires crossing disciplinary boundaries and creating meaningful connections between science, design, culture and lived experience.

Connect knowledge, perception and experience to better understand how environments shape human life.

Research · Perception · Experience · Wellbeing

01 — The Blue Maker

A new ecosystem for designing the life we inhabit

The Blue Maker connects science, architecture, design, art and learning to investigate and transform the relationship between people and the environments they inhabit.

We start from one premise: the environment is not a passive backdrop. It is an active part of our experience, our learning, our wellbeing and our development.

We investigate how spaces can accompany people not only to live better, but also to learn, adapt, create, connect and develop throughout life.

02 — Our Vision

Designing for a life that evolves

People change. They learn. They adapt. They age. They create. They connect. Environments should be able to accompany that evolution too.

The Blue Maker proposes moving from an architecture centred exclusively on the object to an architecture centred on human experience. We design conditions — conditions to perceive, learn, connect, create, move, recover, adapt and age actively.

Signature Frameworks

From research to method

Five named methodologies apply the Blue Maker ecosystem to specific fields — cities, longevity, everyday spaces, art and education, and childhood.

  • Cities

    SenseCity™ — Cities for Healthy Longevity

    Sensitive cities for long, healthy and full lives. An innovative model that integrates Sustainability, Regeneration and Human Sensibility into 21st-century urban design — sustainable cities that are socially and economically resilient, sustentable cities that regenerate their ecosystems and natural resources, sensitive cities that understand and respond to human needs. We design cities that care for people at every stage of life.

    Its seven domains — Health & Longevity, Lifelong Learning, Built Environment, Nature & Regeneration, Community & Purpose, Culture & Senses, and Human Innovation — are measured through the Healthy Longevity Urban Score (HLUS®), an integral tool to evaluate, compare and improve wellbeing and longevity in our cities, and advanced through the 3S® Method — Sensitise: understand people and territories through empathy, active listening and scientific evidence; Synergise: integrate multidisciplinary knowledge, promote collaboration and co-create innovative solutions; Sustain: implement measurable actions, evaluate impact and ensure continuity for long-term wellbeing.

    SenseCity™ is more than a model: it is a global movement to build cities that accompany people through 100 years of life with health, purpose and wellbeing. We design cities today for the lives of tomorrow.

  • Longevity

    AGELESS® — A Methodology for Conscious Longevity

    People, spaces and communities with purpose, wellbeing and vitality at every stage of life. Ageless is not an age. It’s an attitude toward life. AGELESS® integrates neuroarchitecture, environmental psychology, Blue Zones principles and the 3S Method to design lives and environments that foster active, meaningful longevity.

    Its seven pillars: Awareness — becoming conscious of who we are, our beliefs, emotions and purpose, to build a positive vision of the future; Growth — learning, developing and keeping the mind active, creative and open to new possibilities; Environment — designing healthy, biophilic, accessible and stimulating spaces that support physical, mental and emotional wellbeing; Longevity Lifestyle — adopting Blue Zones-inspired habits: movement, nutrition, rest, stress management and connection with nature; Engagement — staying active and contributing our talent through volunteering, flexible work, mentorship or entrepreneurship; Social Connection — cultivating meaningful bonds, support networks and intergenerational relationships that give us belonging and wellbeing; Sense of Purpose — having a clear why, goals that inspire us, and the certainty that our life has meaning and generates impact.

    “The first thing to retire is the word ‘retirement’. What comes next isn’t the end of a story; it’s the chance to write a new chapter.” Applied at every level — AGELESS® homes that promote wellbeing, safety and connection; organisations building intergenerational culture, wellbeing and purpose at work; AGELESS® communities and cities that are inclusive, walkable, green and connected.

  • Wellbeing Spaces

    Nodos Calmos® — A Network of Restorative Spaces

    Points that regulate stress and attention. A network of restorative spaces that favour emotional regulation, reduce stress and improve everyday experience.

  • Art & Education

    Blue Immersion

    It isn’t looked at. It’s walked through. And in that crossing, the body changes rhythm.

    The effect of the colour blue on our brain is perhaps more significant than we might believe. It is the light of our skies, of our seas and oceans, and the hue so prevalent in the world of marketing and advertising. Blue doesn’t only have a relaxing effect — several studies conclude it can improve our physical and psychological health. Blue represents harmony, tranquillity, courtesy and happiness; in its different shades it is read as non-threatening, relaxing and serene, which is why advertising uses it so often, to induce calm, trust and security. Blue has always fascinated the artistic community for one concrete reason: the impact it has on human beings. Wassily Kandinsky used to say that the deeper the blue on a canvas, the more it invites the viewer to think of the infinite.

    Science confirms it: people who spend time in natural spaces where blue light is more intense — sea, lakes, rivers — show greater mental health, and depression rates fall among those who visit these environments regularly. Sea views are associated with lower rates of depression in the elderly, and stress and anxiety fall significantly when we share time with loved ones in spaces where blue is present. Our planet, the Blue Planet, holds the greatest quantity of water. Blue inhabits us.

    This project is born from a question: what if art didn’t represent calm, but produced it? Blue Immersion is a sensory, artistic and educational experience — in cyanotype and mixed media, as an imprint of light and a memory of the body — that connects science, nature and wellbeing to inhabit blue from the body, the environment and life, working across perception (what I feel), environment (where I am) and life (how I inhabit), so that before entering we ask: how does your body feel right now, fast or slow? — and on leaving: did you notice any change?

  • Childhood

    MINC — Integral Neurocompatible Model

    Architecture · Neuroscience · Wellbeing. Learning ecosystems and neurocompatible environments — designing for development, wellbeing and human diversity.

    Why it matters: today’s childhood faces sensory overload, stress and disconnection from nature. The environment directly impacts the developing nervous system. “Not all brains perceive the same way. But all brains are affected by the environment.” Architecture can lower environmental stress and support the development of every child.

    Factors affecting neurodevelopment: digital hyperstimulation, acoustic pollution, visual stress, nature deficit, toxic stress and violence, emotional temperature, poor sleep quality, chaotic environments.

    The five principles of neurocompatible design: 1. Sensory Regulation — reducing overstimulation to lower stress and support self-regulation; 2. Biophilia and Nature — nature regulates, restores and connects; 3. Spatial Neurodiversity — every child needs different spaces for different ways of learning; 4. Biological Rhythms — the environment should accompany the rhythms of body and brain; 5. Safety and Belonging — a predictable, welcoming and loving environment generates regulation.

    Applications: schools and kindergartens, pediatric hospitals, therapeutic centres, spaces for autism, family housing, longevity spaces. Educating for a healthy future means good nutrition in pregnancy and infancy, secure emotional bonds, free play and movement, less screen time at early ages, daily contact with nature, early detection and support, and healthy, toxin-free environments. We design environments that care for human development, integrating architecture, neuroscience and wellbeing to build more conscious and sustainable ecosystems of life.

04 — Blue Nodes

Where the ecosystem becomes reality

Blue Nodes are The Blue Maker’s implementation model — spaces, organisations, communities or territories that embody the ecosystem’s principles to create healthier, more adaptable, connected and human environments.

  • Blue Home. Residential spaces that accompany the different stages of life.
  • Blue Education. Schools and universities that foster learning, creativity and lifelong development.
  • Blue Work. Organisations and workplaces prepared for different generations.
  • Blue Health. Health and rehabilitation spaces centred on people.
  • Blue City. Urban environments that foster movement, autonomy, nature and connection.
  • Blue Culture. Museums and cultural centres where art, perception and wellbeing meet.
  • Blue Hospitality. Hotels and destinations oriented toward wellbeing and longevity.

05 — The Blue Method

From knowledge to impact

  • 01 — Observe. We understand people, behaviours and contexts.
  • 02 — Research. We integrate scientific evidence and interdisciplinary knowledge.
  • 03 — Design. We translate knowledge into spatial and experiential strategies.
  • 04 — Learn. We develop programmes, tools and capabilities.
  • 05 — Experience. We integrate art, perception and sensory experiences.
  • 06 — Implement. We turn the strategy into a Blue Node.
  • 07 — Measure. We evaluate experience, perception, wellbeing and impact.
  • 08 — Evolve. The knowledge generated feeds back into the system so it keeps evolving.

06 — Blue Network

A network of environments that learn

A Blue Node does not function in isolation. It is part of a network of environments connected by shared principles. A person can inhabit, simultaneously: Home → Education → Work → Health → City → Culture → Community. When these environments share the same vision, a new possibility emerges: a culture of wellbeing.
